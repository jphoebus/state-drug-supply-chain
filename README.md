# Who Sets the Price of a Prescription? How Five States Regulate the Drug Supply Chain

How Pennsylvania, Virginia, Ohio, Maryland, and Georgia regulate the businesses between a drug's manufacturer and the patient: pharmacy benefit managers, drug prices, and 340B contract pharmacy arrangements. Current as of October 16, 2026.

**Interactive version:** [jphoebus.github.io/state-drug-supply-chain](https://jphoebus.github.io/state-drug-supply-chain/)

## Headline findings

- **Pharmacy benefit managers:** all five states regulate PBMs through their insurance regulators. The newer laws move from banning specific practices toward setting how PBMs price drugs, usually starting with public plans such as Medicaid and state employee coverage.
- **Drug prices and 340B:** Maryland is the only one of the five with an affordability board or a contract pharmacy law. Legislatures in the other states have debated both. Virginia's legislature passed affordability board bills three years in a row, and governors of both parties vetoed them. On 340B, Ohio enacted reporting by program participants and Virginia ordered a study.

## What stands out

- **PBM rules are converging, at different speeds.** Every state licenses or registers PBMs or will soon (Ohio from July 2027), and states limit practices such as steering. Virginia will require pass-through pricing and full rebate pass-through from 2027; Pennsylvania's 2024 law orders a study of spread pricing.
- **Public plans go first.** Ohio and Virginia use or are moving to a single Medicaid PBM, and Georgia set its pricing rules first for state and local government plans.
- **Drug price authority is the dividing line.** Maryland's board can cap what state and local government plans pay for a drug. Board bills were vetoed in Virginia, did not pass in Georgia, are pending in Ohio, and were not introduced in Pennsylvania this session.
- **340B is moving to the courts and to study.** Maryland's law is in effect while the full Fourth Circuit reconsiders a manufacturer challenge; Virginia created a work group; Ohio required program participants to report.
- **Reporting is the most common tool.** PBM transparency reports in Pennsylvania, manufacturer price reports in Virginia, payment reports in Georgia, and 340B reports in Ohio.

## What to watch

- Pending: the full Fourth Circuit's rehearing of the challenge to Maryland's 340B contract pharmacy law.
- November 1, 2026: Virginia's 340B work group report.
- December 31, 2026: Maryland's PBM workgroup report; Ohio's session ends with HB 890 and the 340B bills in committee.
- January 1, 2027: State and local government purchasers may begin applying Maryland's first upper payment limits (Jardiance, Ozempic), if the regulations are final; required after January 1, 2028.
- 2027 sessions: Virginia affordability board bills; Pennsylvania's HB 2797 on 340B.
- July 1, 2027: Virginia's pass-through pricing requirement and Ohio's PBM license requirement take effect.

The question I expect to shape the next cycle: whether states keep regulating the drug supply chain one link at a time, or start treating pricing across PBMs, manufacturers, and pharmacies as a single decision.

## About the data

The comparison is in [state-drug-supply-chain.csv](state-drug-supply-chain.csv), with one row per state and columns for PBM regulator and license, key PBM rules, 2025–26 PBM changes, affordability board, manufacturer price reporting, 340B contract pharmacy law, sources, and last updated. Facts are drawn from statutes, legislative records, regulator and board pages, and court orders; where a detail rests on a secondary source, the file says "as reported." This comparison describes what states have decided and is not legal or regulatory advice.
